# The Chronometer Balance

Description:
The Chronometer Balance is a mobile application for collectors of horological antiquities, historians of precision timekeeping, and keepers of astronomical observatory heritage, dedicated to cataloging the mechanical instruments used to measure and verify the accuracy of marine chronometers and master clocks that have been superseded by GPS-synced time servers and quartz oscillators. 
Systematize your collection of the tools that "guarded the second"— from heavy brass staking tools and poise scales to specialized depthing tools for gear alignment, from antique watch-timing machines with paper - tape readouts to temperature-compensation testing chambers and astronomical transit telescopes used for stellar time-checks. 
Create a digital archive of temporal precision by recording the instrument type, maker, accuracy rating, era, and the specific observatory or naval shipyard each specimen served. 

## Example Object Fields (Model "Horological Instrument"):

•	Temporal Identifier: Unique code (e.g., "TCB-DENT-1890-LONDON-022") 

•	Instrument Type: Category (staking tool, depthing tool, mainspring winder, poise scale, watch-timing machine, marine chronometer tester) 

•	Manufacturer and Maker: Brand or master clockmaker (E. Dent & Co., Thomas Mercer, Waltham, L.S. Starrett, Bergeon, Kutter) 

•	Country of Origin: Where it was crafted (England, Switzerland, USA, France, Germany) 

•	Era of Production: Decade of manufacture (1850s, 1880s, 1910s, 1940s) 

•	Operating Principle: Mechanical (lever-action), gravity-based (pendulum test), or early electronic (vacuum tube timing) 

•	Precision Rating: Accuracy limits or scale resolution (in seconds per day or microns) 

•	Materials: Primary construction (hardened steel, polished brass, blued steel, mahogany bases, cast iron) 

•	Dimensions and Weight: Physical mass and size (critical for heavy workshop tools) 

•	Condition State: Operational, original finish, jewel integrity (for pivot tools), or museum quality 

•	Included Accessories: Original wooden case, calibration weights, spare collets, or oiling pins 

•	Markings and Stamps: Royal Navy broad arrows, observatory certification marks, or maker's hallmarks 

•	Provenance: Historical context (Naval observatory, chronometer shop, railroad timing station, master watchmaker's estate)
